How the past tests went

The app tries both methods on earlier dates, then checks the winner on later dates it hasn’t used to choose the method.

How the app picks a method

The app compares ridge regression with a simple 14-day average. Regression uses the last 14 days, time of year and how far ahead it’s predicting. It needs at least 365 usable days of non-synthetic data and must lower earlier validation MAE by 2%. The later test scores don’t choose the winner. Error ranges come from past errors at the same forecast lead; they aren’t a guaranteed confidence level. Rain is especially hard to predict from these inputs.

Saved forecast accuracy

Scores need forecasts saved before the day happened, plus weather records loaded afterwards. Two forecasts disagreeing doesn’t tell us which was right. NASA POWER estimates weather over a grid, rather than measuring it at your farm. Its daily cutoffs can also differ from the forecast’s.

METHODS & SOURCES

How this works

Forecasts

The app uses your saved weather to estimate daily rain and average temperature. It tries regression and a 14-day average on earlier dates, then checks the chosen method on later dates. Local station readings are only included if you import them. Open-Meteo uses weather models with more atmospheric information.

Compare the same dates, location and weather measurement. We don’t show percentage errors for temperature in °C. Rainfall MAPE leaves out days below 1 mm, where percentages become misleading; MAE and RMSE still use all usable days.

Data and field records

The Legazpi and Naga samples come from NASA POWER, with their dates and download details kept. Made-up examples are labelled. Your notes are saved for later study; they don’t feed the forecasts or advice.

The location lookup uses saved geoBoundaries maps from 2020 to find the town and province. It can’t check land ownership or measure your field. This version keeps one farm on the local server.
